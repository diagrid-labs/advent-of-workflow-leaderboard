# North Pole Station

> **OPERATIONS BULLETIN 12-01** · NORTH POLE STATION · DIRECTORATE OF GIFT LOGISTICS
> Classification: OPEN · Distribution: ALL ENGINEERING PERSONNEL
> Days to launch window: **23**

Somewhere above the eighty-ninth parallel, under a dome of riveted glass and painted steel, the
North Pole Station still runs the operation exactly the way the atomic age told it to. The
corridors are lined with brass conduit and bakelite switchgear. Letters arrive by pneumatic tube
and leave as punch cards. Harness telemetry comes back on reel-to-reel and is read off green
phosphor screens by elves in silver jumpsuits who stopped taking their helmets off indoors
sometime around 1961.

Out on the north gantry, under floodlights, the sleigh sits fuelled on its launch cradle. It flies
once a year. It has never flown late.

Everything on the station is scheduled by **TINSEL** — Tracking, Inventory, Scheduling and Elf
Logistics — a mainframe that occupies three floors and has been in continuous operation since 1957.
TINSEL decides which letter gets read, which toy gets built, which district gets loaded, and which
reindeer is cleared for flight. For sixty-nine years it has been the only thing standing between
the station and a very quiet December.

## TINSEL is dying

It began with the power dips. A flicker on the main bus, and a job that was halfway through is
simply gone. No error. No record. No memory that it was ever started at all.

The symptoms have been accumulating all autumn. Letters transcribed and then never scored. Crates
sealed against orders nobody placed. A wrapping line held open for three weeks by a booking whose
other half never completed. Last Tuesday the array logged Comet as flight-ready, and Comet has not
been inside the telemetry bay since October.

The obvious answer is to take TINSEL down and rebuild it. The station has looked very carefully at
this and concluded that it cannot. The launch window does not move, the letters do not stop
arriving, and there is no version of December in which the North Pole goes dark for maintenance.

## The rebuild

So the Directorate has settled on the only approach left. The station will be rebuilt subsystem by
subsystem, in place, while it runs. Each subsystem comes off TINSEL and goes onto durable workflows
that survive a restart, remember what they were doing, and can be watched while they do it.

Twelve subsystems. Twelve cut-overs. One every other day until Christmas Eve, when the last one is
not a subsystem at all but the launch itself.

The engineering staff have been told the schedule is comfortable. The engineering staff have
worked here long enough to know what that means.

## You

You have been brought in for the rebuild.

Each challenge hands you one subsystem, tells you what is broken, and leaves the building to you.
The scene sets the problem; everything below it is the specification. You will not need to have
read the previous ones. The station is falling over in twelve independent places at once, which is
the one convenient thing about any of this.

Welcome to North Pole Station. Mind the conduit, it runs hot.
